Bead Count
How the Bead-Count Matrix works
Luminex instruments count individual beads of every analyte's bead region in every well. Low bead counts mean the well × antigen MFI value is derived from very few measurements and is noisier; cells with very low counts shouldn't be trusted at face value. Each cell in the matrix below is coloured by its bead count:
  • Critically Low Bead Count (< {{ bead_thresholds.red_below }})
  • Low Bead Count ({{ bead_thresholds.red_below }}–{{ bead_thresholds.yellow_below - 1 }})
  • Adequate Bead Count (≥ {{ bead_thresholds.yellow_below }})
The four cards summarise the matrix two ways: how many antigens have most of their wells in trouble (suggests a bead-region issue), how many specimens have most of their antigens in trouble (suggests a per-well aspiration / volume problem), and absolute counts of red and yellow cells. An antigen or specimen is flagged when ≥ {{ problem_threshold_pct }}% of its companion cells fall in the red or yellow tiers. All four thresholds ({{ bead_thresholds.red_below }}, {{ bead_thresholds.yellow_below }}, {{ problem_threshold_pct }}%, and the well-fraction cut-off) are editable on the Settings page.

How Background QC works
Background wells are the plate blanks — wells with no specimen (sample name Background0; {{ plate_counts.background }} wells on this plate). This section tracks the blank signal per antigen, this plate vs. previous plates, and shares its plot, hover, and table layout with the Positive and Negative Control QC sections below.
  • Overview plot — two views (toggle buttons top-left, under the legend):
    • x = antigen (xPONENT panel order); y = MFI (log scale).
    • Median ± IQR (default once ≥ 3 past plates exist): a grey bar spans the historical IQR (Q1–Q3) of each antigen's per-plate mean (hover for Q1 / median / Q3); this plate's mean is a dot — blue within the IQR, orange ♦ above/below it (provisional flag).
    • Per-plate data points — each plate as its own dot per antigen; current plate red, past plates along a chronological blue–green gradient (oldest faded, newest past bold). Click legend entries to toggle individual plates; use Show all / Hide past plates for bulk control. (This is the default when there are fewer than 3 past plates, since no IQR exists yet.)
    • Hover any current dot to read this plate's individual well MFIs.
    • A dashed amber line marks the reference max-MFI threshold ({{ bg_max_mfi }}). The plot is fixed-width and scrolls horizontally so all antigens stay legible.
  • Table.
    • One row per antigen: a High CV flag (intra-plate); n wells; one column per Background well (this plate's MFI); the current-plate Mean / SD / %CV / IQR across those wells; the Historical mean / SD / %CV / IQR across each previous plate's mean; and a High hist. CV flag. Click any column header to sort.
    • SD uses n−1; %CV = SD ÷ mean × 100; IQR = Q1–Q3.
    • Two kinds of %CV variability. The current-plate %CV is intra-plate (spread across this plate's Background wells) — flagged amber ◆ above {{ bg_cv_pct }}%. The Historical %CV is inter-assay (spread of each past plate's mean, i.e. run-to-run drift) — flagged ↕ above {{ hist_cv_pct }}% (needs ≥ 2 past plates). The two count cards above summarise each.
    • Rows where this plate's mean falls outside the historical IQR are highlighted — ▲ orange above Q3, ▼ blue below Q1.
  • Hidden tables. Two collapsible checks below the main table: Background well outliers (any single well > mean + 2 SD for its antigen) and Specimens with negative net MFI (specimen MFI minus the plate's mean background for that antigen, where negative).
A reference max-MFI threshold ({{ bg_max_mfi }}, editable on the Settings page) is tracked, but formal Background pass/fail flagging is still in development — no antigens are flagged as failing here yet.

Positive Control QC
How Positive Control QC works
Single-point positive controls (PCs) are samples of known antibody status used to track inter-plate assay performance over time. Unlike the standards (a serial-dilution series fit to a 4PL curve), these are loaded at a single nominal level — here the Cholera High and Cholera Low controls, each run in duplicate wells per plate and not fit to a curve. For any given antigen a control should read at a roughly consistent MFI across runs; the two controls are tracked separately. The plot, hover, and table use the same conventions as Background QC.
  • Cross-plate overview (per control) — two views (toggle buttons top-left, under the legend):
    • x = antigen (panel order), y = MFI (log scale).
    • Median ± IQR (default once ≥ 3 past plates exist): a grey bar spans the historical IQR (Q1–Q3) of each antigen's per-plate mean; this plate's mean (of its duplicate wells) is a dot — blue within the IQR, orange ♦ above/below it.
    • Per-plate data points — each plate as its own dot per antigen; current plate red, past plates on a chronological blue–green gradient (oldest faded, newest bold). Legend entries toggle individual plates; Show all / Hide past plates for bulk control. Default when < 3 past plates exist.
    • Hover a current dot to read its individual well MFIs.
  • Per-control stats table (collapsed).
    • One row per antigen: n wells; one column per well (this plate's MFI); current Mean / SD / %CV / IQR across the wells; Historical mean / SD / %CV / IQR across each past plate's mean; and n past plates.
    • SD uses n−1; %CV = SD ÷ mean × 100; IQR = Q1–Q3.
    • Rows where this plate's mean falls outside the historical IQR are highlighted — ▲ orange above Q3, ▼ blue below Q1.
Formal PC pass/fail flagging is still in development — no antigens are flagged as failing here yet.

Negative Control QC
How Negative Control QC works
A negative control (NC) is a well of pooled seronegative serum (here, pre-2019 North American plasma) that should read antibody-negative for every antigen. Each NC control (e.g. Negative 0 and Negative 49) is tracked separately across plates. The plot, hover, and table use the same conventions as Background and Positive Control QC.
  • Cross-plate overview (per control) — two views (toggle buttons top-left, under the legend):
    • x = antigen (panel order), y = MFI (log scale).
    • Median ± IQR (default once ≥ 3 past plates exist): a grey bar spans the historical IQR (Q1–Q3) of each antigen's per-plate mean; this plate's mean is a dot — blue within the IQR, orange ♦ above/below it.
    • Per-plate data points — each plate as its own dot per antigen; current plate red, past plates on a chronological blue–green gradient (oldest faded, newest bold). Legend entries toggle individual plates; Show all / Hide past plates for bulk control. Default when < 3 past plates exist.
    • Hover a current dot to read its individual well MFIs.
  • Per-control stats table (collapsed).
    • One row per antigen: a Dup. CV flag; n wells; one column per well (this plate's MFI); current Mean / SD / %CV / IQR across the wells; Historical mean / SD / %CV / IQR across each past plate's mean; and n past plates. Click any column header to sort.
    • Duplicate-well outlier check. Each NC control has only two wells per antigen, so a mean+2SD test isn't meaningful. Instead, an antigen is flagged amber ◆ when its two NC wells disagree by more than {{ nc_cv_pct }}% CV — i.e. one well is pulling the average. Sort the Dup. CV column to group them.
A clean NC programme reads low and stable across plates — a point that jumps up flags cross-reactivity, NC contamination, or a bead-region issue. The plot is fixed-width and scrolls horizontally like Background QC. Deeper NC-level QC (per-antigen thresholds, drift flags) is still in development.

What does "Fit OK" mean?
Each antigen's standard points are fit to a 4-parameter logistic (4PL) curve. A fit is marked OK only when all four of these criteria pass:
  • R² ≥ 0.95 on log10(MFI) — the curve actually tracks the data.
  • IC50 inside the tested dilution range (with ±3× margin) — the inflection isn't off the side of the curve.
  • Hill slope between 0.3 and 5.0 — the curve isn't suspiciously flat or step-like.
  • Dynamic range ≥ 3× — upper-plateau MFI is at least 3× the lower-plateau MFI, so there's real signal to fit.
A fit that converges but fails any of these is marked FAIL; the parameters are still shown, but the LLOQ / ULOQ and %-in-range are unreliable.
