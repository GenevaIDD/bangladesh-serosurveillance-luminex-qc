Bead Count
How the Bead-Count Matrix works
Luminex instruments count individual beads of every analyte's bead region in every well. Low bead counts mean the well × antigen MFI value is derived from very few measurements and is noisier; cells with very low counts shouldn't be trusted at face value. Each cell in the matrix below is coloured by its bead count:
  • Critically Low Bead Count (< {{ bead_thresholds.red_below }})
  • Low Bead Count ({{ bead_thresholds.red_below }}–{{ bead_thresholds.yellow_below - 1 }})
  • Adequate Bead Count (≥ {{ bead_thresholds.yellow_below }})
The matrix is antigens (rows) × wells (columns), so each square is one (well × antigen) bead count. The five summary cards slice it along different axes — note the different denominators:
  • Wells with ≥ 1 antigen at critically low bead count — counts wells (out of the {{ bead_n_wells }} wells run) that have at least one antigen below {{ bead_thresholds.red_below }} beads. This is the most sensitive well-level indicator, as it flags a well even when only a single antigen falls below the threshold; it helps identify wells that may warrant review or re-running.
  • Overall grid cells < {{ bead_thresholds.red_below }} and {{ bead_thresholds.red_below }}–{{ bead_thresholds.yellow_below - 1 }} — count individual grid cells (well × antigen squares, out of wells × antigens ≈ the whole grid) in the critically-low and low tiers. These are the raw magnitude of low-count measurements, not a count of wells or antigens.
  • Antigens flagged — counts antigens (rows) where ≥ {{ problem_threshold_pct }}% of that antigen's wells are red or yellow (suggests a bead-region issue affecting many wells).
  • Specimens flagged — counts specimens/wells (columns) where ≥ {{ problem_threshold_pct }}% of that well's antigens are red or yellow (suggests a per-well aspiration / volume problem).
A given number of low cells may reflect a single failed well (affecting many antigens) or many isolated single-cell events; the well- and antigen-level cards distinguish these cases. All thresholds ({{ bead_thresholds.red_below }}, {{ bead_thresholds.yellow_below }}, and {{ problem_threshold_pct }}%) are editable on the Settings page.

How Background QC works
Background wells are the plate blanks — wells with no specimen (sample name Background0; {{ plate_counts.background }} wells on this plate). This section tracks the blank signal per antigen, this plate vs. previous plates, and shares its plot, hover, and table layout with the Positive and Negative Control QC sections below.
  • Overview plot — two views (toggle buttons top-left, under the legend):
    • x = antigen (xPONENT panel order); y = MFI (log scale).
    • Median ± IQR (default once ≥ 3 past plates exist): a grey bar spans the historical IQR (Q1–Q3) of each antigen's per-plate mean (hover for Q1 / median / Q3); this plate's mean is a dot — blue within the IQR, orange ♦ above/below it (provisional flag).
    • Per-plate data points — each plate as its own dot per antigen; current plate red, past plates along a chronological blue–green gradient (oldest faded, newest past bold). Click legend entries to toggle individual plates; use Show all / Hide past plates for bulk control. (This is the default when there are fewer than 3 past plates, since no IQR exists yet.)
    • Hover any current dot to read this plate's individual well MFIs.
    • A dashed amber line marks the reference max-MFI threshold ({{ bg_max_mfi }}). The plot is fixed-width and scrolls horizontally so all antigens stay legible.
  • Table.
    • One row per antigen: an Intra-assay %CV flag; n wells; one column per Background well (this plate's MFI); the current-plate Mean / SD / %CV / IQR across those wells; the Historical mean / SD / IQR and Inter-assay %CV across each previous plate's mean; and an Inter-assay %CV flag. Click any column header to sort.
    • SD uses n−1; %CV = SD ÷ mean × 100; IQR = Q1–Q3.
    • Two kinds of %CV variability. Intra-assay %CV is the spread across this plate's Background wells — flagged amber ◆ above {{ bg_cv_pct }}%. Inter-assay %CV is the spread of each past plate's mean (run-to-run drift) — flagged ↕ above {{ hist_cv_pct }}% (needs ≥ 2 past plates). The two count cards above summarise each. The same two flags appear in Positive and Negative Control QC. Both thresholds are editable on the Settings page (QC thresholds).
    • Rows where this plate's mean falls outside the historical IQR are highlighted — ▲ orange above Q3, ▼ blue below Q1.
  • Hidden tables. Two collapsible checks below the main table: Background well outliers (any single well > mean + 2 SD for its antigen) and Specimens with negative net MFI (specimen MFI minus the plate's mean background for that antigen, where negative).
A reference max-MFI threshold ({{ bg_max_mfi }}, editable on the Settings page) is tracked, but formal Background pass/fail flagging is still in development — no antigens are flagged as failing here yet.

What this checks, in plain terms. Each antigen is measured in the {{ plate_counts.background }} blank Background wells on this plate. Normally those {{ plate_counts.background }} readings agree. This table catches the case where one well reads much higher than the others and drags the average background up for that antigen.
  • For each well we take the other wells of that antigen, compute their mean and SD, and flag the well if it sits more than 2 SD above that mean (a "leave-one-out" test). The LOO columns show those numbers and decide the flag.

With only ~4 wells the suspect well inflates its own SD, so this rarely trips — the "all-wells flag" usually reads "no" even for a real outlier. It's shown only for comparison; the LOO flag is the one to trust.

Positive Control QC
How Positive Control QC works
Single-point positive controls (PCs) are samples of known antibody status used to track inter-plate assay performance over time. Unlike the standards (a serial-dilution series fit to a {{ curve_model_label }} curve), these are loaded at a single nominal level — here the Cholera High and Cholera Low controls, each run in duplicate wells per plate and not fit to a curve. For any given antigen a control should read at a roughly consistent MFI across runs; the two controls are tracked separately. The plot, hover, and table use the same conventions as Background QC.
  • Cross-plate overview (per control) — two views (toggle buttons top-left, under the legend):
    • x = antigen (panel order), y = MFI (log scale).
    • Median ± IQR (default once ≥ 3 past plates exist): a grey bar spans the historical IQR (Q1–Q3) of each antigen's per-plate mean; this plate's mean (of its duplicate wells) is a dot — blue within the IQR, orange ♦ above/below it.
    • Per-plate data points — each plate as its own dot per antigen; current plate red, past plates on a chronological blue–green gradient (oldest faded, newest bold). Legend entries toggle individual plates; Show all / Hide past plates for bulk control. Default when < 3 past plates exist.
    • Hover a current dot to read its individual well MFIs.
  • Per-control stats table (collapsed).
    • One row per antigen: an Intra-assay %CV flag (across the control's duplicate wells); n wells; one column per well (this plate's MFI); current Mean / SD / %CV / IQR across the wells; Historical mean / SD / IQR and Inter-assay %CV across each past plate's mean, with an Inter-assay %CV flag; and n past plates. Click any column header to sort.
    • SD uses n−1; %CV = SD ÷ mean × 100; IQR = Q1–Q3. Intra-assay %CV flags a control whose duplicate wells disagree by > {{ nc_cv_pct }}%; Inter-assay %CV flags run-to-run drift > {{ hist_cv_pct }}% (matching Background/NC). Both thresholds are editable on the Settings page (QC thresholds).
    • Rows where this plate's mean falls outside the historical IQR are highlighted — ▲ orange above Q3, ▼ blue below Q1.
Formal PC pass/fail flagging is still in development — no antigens are flagged as failing here yet.

Negative Control QC
How Negative Control QC works
A negative control (NC) is a well of pooled seronegative serum (here, pre-2019 North American plasma) that should read antibody-negative for every antigen. Each NC control (e.g. Negative 0 and Negative 49) is tracked separately across plates. The plot, hover, and table use the same conventions as Background and Positive Control QC.
  • Cross-plate overview (per control) — two views (toggle buttons top-left, under the legend):
    • x = antigen (panel order), y = MFI (log scale).
    • Median ± IQR (default once ≥ 3 past plates exist): a grey bar spans the historical IQR (Q1–Q3) of each antigen's per-plate mean; this plate's mean is a dot — blue within the IQR, orange ♦ above/below it.
    • Per-plate data points — each plate as its own dot per antigen; current plate red, past plates on a chronological blue–green gradient (oldest faded, newest bold). Legend entries toggle individual plates; Show all / Hide past plates for bulk control. Default when < 3 past plates exist.
    • Hover a current dot to read its individual well MFIs.
  • Per-control stats table (collapsed).
    • One row per antigen: an Intra-assay %CV flag; n wells; one column per well (this plate's MFI); current Mean / SD / %CV / IQR across the wells; Historical mean / SD / IQR and Inter-assay %CV across each past plate's mean, with an Inter-assay %CV flag; and n past plates. Click any column header to sort.
    • Intra-assay %CV (duplicate-well) check. Each NC control has only two wells per antigen, so a mean+2SD test isn't meaningful. Instead, an antigen is flagged amber ◆ when its two NC wells disagree by more than {{ nc_cv_pct }}% CV — i.e. one well is pulling the average. Inter-assay %CV (between-plate drift, flagged ↕ above {{ hist_cv_pct }}%) is shown too, matching Background/PC. Both thresholds are editable on the Settings page (QC thresholds).
A clean NC programme reads low and stable across plates — a point that jumps up flags cross-reactivity, NC contamination, or a bead-region issue. The plot is fixed-width and scrolls horizontally like Background QC. Deeper NC-level QC (per-antigen thresholds, drift flags) is still in development.

Definition of "Fit OK"
Each antigen's standard points are fit to this report's model ({{ curve_model_name }}). A fit is marked OK only when all of these criteria pass:
  • R² ≥ 0.95 on log10(MFI) — the curve actually tracks the data.
  • IC50 inside the tested dilution range (with ±3× margin) — the inflection isn't off the side of the curve.
  • Hill slope between 0.3 and 5.0 — the curve isn't suspiciously flat or step-like.
  • Dynamic range ≥ 3× — upper-plateau MFI is at least 3× the lower-plateau MFI, so there's real signal to fit.
The Fit-OK column has three states:
  • OK — the curve converged and passed all criteria above.
  • FAIL — a curve was fit but failed at least one criterion; its parameters are still shown, but the LLOQ / ULOQ and %-in-range are unreliable.
  • NO_FIT — no curve could be fit at all (no signal, too few dilution points, or the fit did not converge); the parameters are blank and the antigen cannot be quantified in this report.

One fit table per standard pool. Every antigen's fit against that pool is listed — nothing is dropped. The Relevance column marks whether that pool is the antigen's designated calibrator/reference (independent of fit quality): relevant rows are shown first and highlighted, other antigens (fit against this pool only as a best-fit fallback) are muted. Click any column header to sort ascending/descending. (% in range is a single-pool scoring metric and is omitted here.)
